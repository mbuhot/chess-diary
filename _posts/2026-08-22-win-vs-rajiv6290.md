### Game Overview

This game was all about prioritising development and activity over material, particularly pawns. Black gave up the e5 pawn in the opening and then played an early queen attack line. That forced me to either give the pawn back or trade a knight for a bishop. I took the trade, and came out of it with two pawns in the centre and a knight developed while my opponent had nothing out but the queen.

It was pretty clear my opponent likes to play with the queen. Generally the way to punish that is to maximise development and go for an attack, so that's what I did. The plan was simple: a bishop and queen battery on c2 and d3, then fire away at h7. By the time it landed I had happily given away four pawns.

### Give the Pawn Back, or Trade? (Moves 4-5)

**4.c3 Bxc3 5.Nxc3 Qxe5**

The queen on e7 gave me a choice. I could retreat the knight and hand the pawn straight back, or I could exchange a knight for a bishop. I opted for the exchange. **4.c3** attacked the bishop, and after **4...Bxc3 5.Nxc3** my b1 knight came into the game on its way to the fight. Black took back on e5. Material is level: I traded a c-pawn for an e-pawn, which is generally a favourable swap. More to the point, my d-pawn is ready to come to d4 and give me the centre.

### Engine Review

The analysis doesn't think this was a very accurate game at all. In fact it's one of the lowest ratings I've seen: 250 for me against 150 for my opponent, and 46% accuracy for me against 35% for my opponent.

I did miss a chance to win my opponent's queen outright. When the queen took on d4 I could have played **13.Bxh7+** immediately. The bishop leaves d3 with check, which opens the d-file for my queen on d1, and after **13...Kxh7 14.Qxd4** the black queen is gone.

<figure>
  <img src="{{ '/assets/images/2026-08-22-game1/engine-line-bxh7-wins-queen.gif' | relative_url }}" alt="Animated engine line: 13.Bxh7+ Kxh7 14.Qxd4">
  <figcaption>13.Bxh7+ Kxh7 14.Qxd4: the check clears the d-file and the queen falls.</figcaption>
</figure>

Stockfish puts that line at 6.1 pawns for White. The move I played, **13.g3**, drops it to 1.3 in Black's favour. That's one I'll need to keep an eye out for.

The battery itself was sound. At move 16, **Qd3** is Stockfish's first choice in the position. What lost the game for Black was **15...Bxh3** followed by **16...Bxf1**. Grabbing the h-pawn cost about a pawn and a half of evaluation, and taking the rook allowed mate in one. **16...g6** holds the position and leaves Black comfortably better.

### Reflections

I'm actually happy with this game despite the engine rating. I think the engine just really doesn't like this style of play: gobbling up pawns with the queen while the other pieces are undeveloped, and sacrificing pawns to go for an attack. It was the first real game of the day and I came away with a win, so I'm really happy.

**What went well:**

- Reading the opponent's habit. They wanted to play with the queen, so I maximised development and attacked.
- A simple, concrete plan. Bishop to c2, queen to d3, fire at h7.
- Giving up four pawns without flinching, because activity was worth more than the material.
- Ignoring the threat to my rook on f1 when there was mate in one on the board.

**What to work on:**

- Bishop checks on h7 that clear a file or a diagonal behind them. **13.Bxh7+** won a queen and I walked past it.

### Game Overview

This game was all about recognising that a locked centre meant a kingside pawn storm was safe. That included opening up the h-file and invading from the flank.

It was a pretty high accuracy game. I'm very happy with 85% from chess.com and a game rating of 1650. No blunders and no mistakes, but there is one missed opportunity that we'll have a look at.

---

### A Passive Pirc (Moves 3-7)

<figure>
  <img src="{{ '/assets/images/2026-08-22-game2/move04.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Position after 2...a6, White to play">

**3.Nf3 c6 4.Bd3 Bg4 5.O-O e6 6.Nc3 Be7 7.h3 Bh5**

Technically this was a Pirc Defense, because Black opened with **1...d6**. It didn't go into the Pirc lines I'm familiar with playing, because their second move was **2...a6**, which is a super passive way to play it. They developed their bishops. I developed my knight and my light-squared bishop, and castled.

### Offering the Lock (Moves 8-9)

<figure>
  <img src="{{ '/assets/images/2026-08-22-game2/move14.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Position after 7...Bh5, White to play">
  <figcaption>After 7...Bh5: the bishop sits on the h5-d1 diagonal, Black's king still in the centre.</figcaption>
</figure>

**8.e5 d5 9.g4 Bg6**

**8.e5** offered Black the chance to either open up the centre or lock it. Black chose **8...d5**, locking it. Then I thought that this is going to be fun. I played **9.g4**, attacking the bishop that was sitting on h5, and started a pawn storm on the kingside to see what I could break open. The bishop retreated to g6.

---

### Trading on g6 (Move 10)

<figure>
  <img src="{{ '/assets/images/2026-08-22-game2/move18.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Position after 9...Bg6, White to play">
  <figcaption>After 9...Bg6: the two light-squared bishops face each other.</figcaption>
</figure>

**10.Bxg6 hxg6**

I played the second best move here, immediately taking on g6 with my own light-squared bishop. I could see that this was going to require Black to recapture with one of the pawns, weakening the area where Black was probably going to castle. Black recaptured **10...hxg6**, which cleared the h-file for the rook.

### The Rook Comes In (Moves 11-13)

<figure>
  <img src="{{ '/assets/images/2026-08-22-game2/move20.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Position after 10...hxg6, White to play">

**11.Bf4 Rxh3 12.Kg2 Rh8 13.Rh1**

Black came in with **11...Rxh3**. At first it looks like it's my king that's losing safety, but this is all part of the plan. I played **12.Kg2**, allowing my rook, my queen, and eventually my a1 rook to scoot around the back rank and then invade on the h-file. The black rook went back to h8 and I put mine on h1.

### The Knight on h6 (Moves 14-15)

<figure>
  <img src="{{ '/assets/images/2026-08-22-game2/move26.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Position after 13...Nh6, White to play">
  <figcaption>After 13...Nh6: the knight blocks the h-file, and my g4 pawn can hit it.</figcaption>
</figure>

**14.g5 Nd7 15.gxh6 gxh6**

After putting my rook on h1, Black decided to block its access to the eighth rank by playing **13...Nh6**. This is a mistake, because that knight can be attacked with a pawn push. I played **14.g5**. Black gave up the knight, and I captured with the pawn, **15.gxh6**. After the exchanges there was a pawn sitting on h6.

---

### Bishop to g7 (Moves 16-18)

<figure>
  <img src="{{ '/assets/images/2026-08-22-game2/move30.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Position after 15...gxh6, White to play">
  <figcaption>After 15...gxh6: a black pawn on h6 blocks the file.</figcaption>
</figure>

**16.Bxh6 g5 17.Bg7 Rxh1 18.Qxh1**

I decided to capture the h6 pawn with my bishop, inviting Black to take my bishop with their rook and allowing me to recapture with my own rook. Black didn't capture the bishop, so instead I moved the bishop to **17.Bg7**, attacking Black's rook. Black either needed to exchange rooks and give up control of the h-file, or move the rook to g8, where I would be able to pin it on the eighth rank with **Rh8**. Black decided to exchange rooks, I recaptured with my queen, and there was a clear path for the queen to invade, getting to h8 with check.

### The Missed Mate (Move 19)

<figure>
  <img src="{{ '/assets/images/2026-08-22-game2/move36.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Position after 18...f6, White to play">
  <figcaption>After 18...f6: the queen on h1 has the whole h-file.</figcaption>

**19.Qh8+ Nf8**

On move 18 Black blundered checkmate in one, and I missed it. The correct move was **19.Qh5#**. That would have won the game on the spot. Never mind. I played **19.Qh8+** and Black blocked with the knight.

**20.Rh1 Kd7 21.exf6 Ng6 22.Qxd8+ Rxd8**

I decided I'd bring another piece into the attack, because I hadn't quite calculated a checkmate sequence. The bishop on e7 seemed to be doing a good job protecting the king. Then the king started to run away, and I felt like maybe I'd been too slow. So I took on f6 with my pawn, looking to at least pick up some material. Black hopped the knight forward to g6, attacking my queen, so I went for the queen exchange.

### The Seventh Rank (Moves 24-26)

<figure>
  <img src="{{ '/assets/images/2026-08-22-game2/move46.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Position after 23...Nxe7, White to play">
  <figcaption>After 23...Nxe7: the black king sits on d7 with my bishop already on g7.</figcaption>

**24.Rh7 Nf5 25.Bf6+ Kc8 26.Bxd8**

**24.Rh7** brought the rook in on the seventh rank, aimed straight at the king on d7. My own bishop on g7 was the barrier in the way, which meant any bishop move would be a discovered check. Black moved the knight to f5, and **25.Bf6+** was the discovered check, attacking the rook on d8 at the same time. The king stepped to c8, I took the rook, and Black resigned. I was already up five points of material, and this took me to plus seven.

### Engine Review

The computer analysis doesn't really give much additional insight other than the missed checkmate in one.

The evaluation chart looks pretty steady right up until **13...Nh6**. That's where it started to fall apart for them. Then it completely crumbled when they played **18...f6**, giving me the mate in one opportunity. With my queen on h1, the h-file completely empty and the f7 pawn now on f6, **19.Qh5** delivers mate down the h5-e8 diagonal. My bishop on g7 covers f8, and Black's own knight and bishop take away d7 and e7.

### Reflections

All in all this went well. The strategy of developing quickly, taking space in the centre, and not being afraid to attack once the pieces are developed is working really well.

What made this game special was having the confidence to push the pawns in front of my king where it was the right strategy, given the centre was locked and the opponent hadn't castled yet. It looked like there was a good chance to invade and attack the king before they could even develop the queen.

**What went well:**

- Reading the locked centre and knowing the kingside pawn storm was safe.
- Forcing the recapture on g6 with a pawn, wrecking the squares Black wanted to castle into.
- Walking the king to g2 so all three heavy pieces could reach the h-file.
- The discovered check on the seventh rank to win the exchange.

**What to work on:**

- Look for all the available checks. In this game one of them was actually a checkmate.

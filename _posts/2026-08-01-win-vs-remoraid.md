### Game Overview

I was feeling a little rusty and dropped the first few blitz games to blunders and timeouts, but this one was the highlight of the session. It was a very high accuracy game for me and it finished with a quick checkmate. Black blundered a piece early, then castled straight into a queen and bishop battery aimed at h7. I peeled away the defender, opened the diagonal, and the queen crashed through for mate on move 17.

---

### The Opening (Moves 1-8)

**1. e4 e5 2. Nf3 d6 3. d4 exd4 4. Nxd4**

<figure>
  <img src="{{ '/assets/images/2026-08-01-game1/move07.png' | relative_url }}" alt="After 4. Nxd4">
  <figcaption>After 4. Nxd4: the centre opens up and I recapture with the knight.</figcaption>
</figure>

I was going for the Scotch-style d4 break I've been practising. Black met me with **2...d6**, which makes it a Philidor. A true Scotch needs **2...Nc6**, but I still got the open central position I wanted. After **3. d4 exd4** I took back with the knight and had a comfortable, open game to develop into.

**4...Bd7 5. Bd3 Nc6 6. Nf3 Bg4 7. O-O Nf6 8. h3**

<figure>
  <img src="{{ '/assets/images/2026-08-01-game1/move15.png' | relative_url }}" alt="After 8. h3">
  <figcaption>After 8. h3: bishop developed to d3, king castled, and h3 questions the g4 bishop.</figcaption>
</figure>

I was happy with how the pieces came out. The bishop went to d3, already sitting on the b1-h7 diagonal, I got my king castled, and I had a nice strong pawn on e4. Then I played **h3** to take g4 away from Black: it put the question to the bishop that had come to g4 and denied that square to the knight on f6 at the same time.

---

### Winning a Piece (Moves 8-11)

**8...Bxf3 9. Qxf3 Nd4 10. Qe3 Nxc2**

<figure>
  <img src="{{ '/assets/images/2026-08-01-game1/move20.png' | relative_url }}" alt="After 10...Nxc2">
  <figcaption>After 10...Nxc2: the knight forks my queen and rook, but it's hanging.</figcaption>
</figure>

Black traded the bishop for my knight, dropped the other knight into d4, and then grabbed the pawn on c2. At first glance **10...Nxc2** looks appealing because it forks my queen on e3 and my rook on a1. The catch is that the knight is hanging: my bishop on d3 covers c2.

**11. Bxc2**

<figure>
  <img src="{{ '/assets/images/2026-08-01-game1/move21.png' | relative_url }}" alt="After 11. Bxc2">
  <figcaption>After 11. Bxc2: I'm up a piece and the bishop is back on the diagonal to h7.</figcaption>
</figure>

I took the knight and came out a piece up. As a bonus, the bishop landed on c2, right back on the b1-h7 diagonal and pointing straight at where Black's king was about to sit.

---

### Setting Up the Battery (Moves 11-15)

**11...Be7 12. Nc3 O-O 13. Qd3**

<figure>
  <img src="{{ '/assets/images/2026-08-01-game1/move25.png' | relative_url }}" alt="After 13. Qd3">
  <figcaption>After 13. Qd3: queen and bishop line up on the diagonal into h7.</figcaption>
</figure>

Two moves later Black castled, and I could already see the tactic. The bishop on c2 was eyeing the h7 pawn in front of the king. My queen was on the third rank, so it was a short hop to **Qd3**, dropping in front of the bishop to form the battery aimed at that weak h7 square. Before it could land, though, I had to deal with the knight on f6, which was guarding h7.

**13...c6 14. Bg5**

<figure>
  <img src="{{ '/assets/images/2026-08-01-game1/move27.png' | relative_url }}" alt="After 14. Bg5">
  <figcaption>After 14. Bg5: attacking the f6 knight, the defender of h7.</figcaption>
</figure>

**Bg5** went after the knight on f6, the piece holding h7 together.

**14...h6 15. Bxf6 Bxf6**

<figure>
  <img src="{{ '/assets/images/2026-08-01-game1/move30.png' | relative_url }}" alt="After 15...Bxf6">
  <figcaption>After 15...Bxf6: the defender of h7 is gone.</figcaption>
</figure>

Black played **h6** to kick the bishop, but I had no intention of keeping it. It was there purely to take the knight, so **15. Bxf6** removed the guard and Black recaptured with the bishop.

### The Breakthrough (Moves 16-17)

**16. e5**

<figure>
  <img src="{{ '/assets/images/2026-08-01-game1/move31.png' | relative_url }}" alt="After 16. e5">
  <figcaption>After 16. e5: the pawn clears e4 off the diagonal and pokes the bishop on f6.</figcaption>
</figure>

**e5** cleared the way for the battery. On the surface it attacks the bishop on f6, but the real point is that it shifts the e4 pawn off the diagonal, opening the road for the queen to reach h7 with mate. To stay in the game Black has to give up the bishop.

**16...dxe5 17. Qh7#**

<figure>
  <img src="{{ '/assets/images/2026-08-01-game1/move33.png' | relative_url }}" alt="After 17. Qh7#">
  <figcaption>After 17. Qh7#: the queen lands on h7, guarded by the bishop on c2. Checkmate.</figcaption>
</figure>

Black saved the bishop with **16...dxe5** and missed the threat entirely. **17. Qh7#** finished it. The queen sits next to the king, defended by the bishop on c2, and there is no square to run to.

### Engine Review

This was 95.6% accuracy for me, which I believe is a personal best, against 69.7% for Black. The game rating came out at 1350 for me versus 750 for my opponent. I had no misses and no blunders. Black had two mistakes and one blunder.

The blunder was **10...Nxc2**, which dropped the knight. Beyond that, the game really turned on Black's failure to defend against the attack, and the engine flags two moments.

The first was **14...h6**. The engine prefers **14...g6**, which makes sense: it blunts the whole attack coming in on the light squares from the queen and bishop. If Black had played **g6**, I would have had to change my plan and go after the dark squares instead.

The second was **16...dxe5**. Here the engine gives only two moves that avoid an immediate mate in one. One is **16...g6** again, blocking the diagonal, which gives up the bishop and leaves me up two pieces with an overwhelming position. The other is **16...Re8**, when after **17. Qh7+** the king can escape to f8 and the pawn takes the bishop on f6. That again leaves me up two pieces with the queen infiltrating on the seventh rank and a winning position. From there it would have been a matter of centralising the rooks and trading down the remaining pieces.

### Reflections

**What went well:**

- This one was all about seeing the shape of the attack several moves ahead: the queen and bishop battery, eliminating the defender on f6, pushing the pawn to open the diagonal, and going for it. I don't think there's anything I'd play differently in this game.
- The accuracy. A clean game with no misses or blunders, and a personal best number to go with it.

**What to work on:**

- Warming up faster. I dropped the first few games of the session to blunders and timeouts before I found my rhythm, so I'd like to be this sharp from the first game rather than needing a few to shake off the rust.

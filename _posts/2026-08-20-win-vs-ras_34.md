### Game Overview

I went with an e4 opening and Black took us into a Philidor Defense. I traded a knight for a bishop on f3, and by move eight the engine had me up 3.2. We opened up the centre, Black checked my king with the queen on e7, and I blocked the check with my own queen. We castled on opposite sides, I traded rooks on f8 and picked up a pawn on e4. Then some endgame technique to finish: a queenside majority, one passed pawn on the b-file, and a resignation on move 33.

### Engine Review

The engine analysis is interesting. Apparently **9.Qc3** was a mistake. It dropped the evaluation from 3.1 to 0.9. I was planning on using that queen for the line **Bxc6 bxc6 Qxc6+**, giving me a very active queen.

The better move is actually **9.Qb3**, which forms a battery with the bishop on d5 and can be used to check the king with the bishop going all the way in on f7, forcing the king to move and preventing castling. Stockfish gives **9.Qb3 Qc7 10.Be3 Nge7 11.Bf7+ Kd8** at 3.3. There's no material win or fancy tactic in it. I think that's what the engine is evaluating as better: the king can't castle and has to sit in the middle.

**14.Qe3** also cost about a pawn of evaluation. The engine preferred **14.Kd1**, keeping the queens on. I was happy to trade.

After **33.Rc8+** it's mate in eight. The longest defence is **33...Bd8 34.Rxb8 Ke7 35.Rxd8 Kxd8 36.b8=Q+**, and the queen and knight run the king down from there.

### Reflections

**What went well:**

- The opening. I'm pretty suspicious of the Philidor. I feel like it's one of the more passive openings for Black, and that makes it hard to play well.
- Being solid after the queen trade. No blunders once the position simplified.
- Using the single pawn advantage to promote and win the game. Sometimes that's all it takes: not blundering and being up a pawn.

**What to work on:**

- Queen placement in the opening. **9.Qc3** had a plan behind it, but **9.Qb3** does more with the same tempo. It builds the battery with the bishop and takes castling away from Black.

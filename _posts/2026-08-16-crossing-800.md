### Overview

This is a different kind of post. Instead of one game reviewed in depth, here are twenty three rapid games played across an afternoon and evening, with a single paragraph and the final position for each one.

My win rate wasn't great and I made a lot of mistakes, but somewhere in the middle of the session I could tell I was in with a chance of crossing 800 if I could just avoid blundering my queen as often as my opponents were blundering theirs. I started the day on 780, dropped to 757 after six games, and the last game took me from 795 to 803.

The engine numbers below are Stockfish evaluations in pawns, from my point of view: **+3.0** means I'm winning by about three pawns of material, **-3.0** means my opponent is. I've noted the biggest advantage each side reached before the game was decided, because the pattern of this session is right there. Six of my ten losses came from positions where I was better or completely winning, and four of my thirteen wins came from positions where I was losing.

### Game 1: Loss vs tuka2109 (768) as White

An even opening, and then **8. Nc3** which simply hangs the knight to the pawn on d4. The engine had the position at level before that move and at nearly five pawns down after it. Then, having taken on b7 with the queen and let Black attack it with **13...Rb8**, I played **15. Rd1** and left the queen where it stood: **15...Rxb7** and the game was over. Two pieces given away in seven moves, and I resigned a queen and a rook down.

### Reflections

Twenty three games is a lot of data about my own habits, and the pattern is not subtle. Ten losses, and in six of them I had a better or completely winning position first: Games 5, 6, 8, 16, 19 and 20. Four of my wins came from positions where I was losing: Games 11, 17, 22 and 23. At this rating the games are not being decided by opening knowledge or by plans. They are decided by whoever stops giving material away first.

**What went well:**

- The queen blunders went both ways, and I came out ahead on the count. Games 6, 11, 14 and 18 all turned on my opponent's queen, and I only had to spot it and take it.
- Finishing the attack. Six of the thirteen wins ended in checkmate rather than resignation, including three in the last seven games.
- Not tilting after the bad run. Losing four of the first six and dropping to 757 could easily have ended the session there.

**What to work on:**

- The one move check before moving anything. Games 1, 8, 16 and 20 were all one move: a knight next to a pawn, a king to the wrong square, a castle onto an open file, a queen next to a knight.
- Looking at what the opponent just did. Game 6 is the clearest case of the session: **19...Qe3+** put a queen on a square my own bishop was attacking and I saw a check instead of a free queen.
- Converting. Games 8 and 16 were both won on material and both ended in checkmate against me.
